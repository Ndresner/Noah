# Ford / Lincoln – Warranty & Policy Information §5.1, 5.2, 7.3 + OWS User Guide (summary)

Source: Ford Motor Company "Warranty & Policy Information" manual (Ford and Lincoln combined; published 10-Nov-2025, last updated 15-Nov-2026) and the ONE Warranty Solution (OWS) User Guide V10.18 (August 2026), received Nov 2026 for Findlay Lincoln (NV). Copyrighted dealer documents – summary and short quotes only; originals are not stored in the repo. Page numbers are PDF pages.

## Key points
- 5.1.01.06 (p.137): Ford's policy is to pay warranty labor at an hourly rate that is "fair, reasonable, and competitive," administered through one of two labor rate request options (5.1.01.07).
- 5.1.01.08 (p.138): labor = Ford Service Labor Time Standards hours x the dealer's approved warranty labor rate "in effect on the date of repair." OWS extends the rate automatically (Hrs x Labor Rate, OWS p.34); the dealer does not key the rate on the claim.
- 5.1.02.11 (p.142): parts = Dealer Price (or Exchange Price) in effect on the date of repair + parts allowance. "Unless otherwise specified by Ford in writing, the parts allowance on Ford-paid repairs is 40%." OWS applies it automatically at Pre-Validation (OWS p.33).
- No parts markup increase procedure exists anywhere in the manual or OWS guide. The 40% is a flat default; any change would have to come through the state statute.
- State-law deferral is scattered and generic (see below); there is no "as an alternative to your state statute" statement like Honda 6.15.

## Labor rate options (5.1.01.07, p.137–138)
- **Index Rate Option:** three-year agreement; rate rises automatically each June 1 by the change in national CPI plus a 0.5% service cost adjustment. Enroll online in DPES during the May enrollment window. Once signed, the warranty rate "is no longer dependent on the Dealer's retail labor rate." Check whether Findlay Lincoln is currently enrolled – an active Index agreement may need to be exited before a statutory filing (agreement terms not in the manual).
- **Retail Rate Option:** warranty rate equal to the dealer's "effective retail rate," provided it is fair, reasonable and competitive. The dealer must show:
  - a retail rate consistent with sound business practices;
  - a consistent retail pricing practice with a retail labor rate at least equal to the requested warranty rate;
  - a consistent practice of listing actual or standard hours (to the tenth) per repair on the office copy of the RO (hours on the customer copy optional "unless required by state or local law or regulation").
- Approved increases are "effective on a going-forward basis only." Requests go to the Parts & Service Zone Manager.
- **Not in the manual:** RO sample size, look-back window, who picks the start RO, qualifying/excluded repairs, forms, competitive survey, verification steps, response time or frequency limit. Ford presumably handles these outside the manual (Zone / regional guidance) – ask the Zone Manager for the current Retail Rate Option worksheet before relying on any of it.
- Secondary P&A code (5.1.01.06): LimoCare-certified dealers and dealers servicing 12+ motor homes a year can carry a separate rate; motor home rate starts at the standard warranty rate and can be raised through the Retail Rate Option.

## Parts reimbursement detail (5.1.02, p.138–143)
- Default 40% parts allowance on Dealer Price / Exchange Price; batteries are paid at a net price that already includes the allowance (5.1.02.02).
- Engine and transmission exchange assemblies: net price + fixed handling allowance, no parts markup (5.1.02.13; OWS p.73–75, chart eff. 1 July 2024). HV traction batteries also get a fixed handling allowance (OWS).
- Tires: FCSD Dealer price + 25% (5.1.02.17).
- Paint material: paint labor hours x $54.70, plus the normal parts markup (example uses 40%) (5.1.02.10).
- Outside-purchased parts: actual cost + allowance, capped at Dealer Price of the comparable Ford part + allowance; price difference (PRDIFF) on emergency repairs paid at cost with no markup (5.1.02.09).
- Sublet / outside labor: capped at the labor-op amount at the dealer's approved warranty rate "unless authorized by law" (5.1.01.13). Dealer-owned sublet facilities must claim labor ops at the warranty rate.
- Shop supplies not reimbursable (3.5.02.70). Ford/Lincoln Loyalty Program repairs "must be at warranty rates" (5.2.03.04).
- Dealer may submit a part at NET by entering the markup amount as dealer participation (OWS p.33).

## State-law language
- 3.2.01 (p.51): states "have, or may enact, laws" affecting dealer business; manual doesn't list them; Ford recommends dealers consult legal counsel. No statement that the statute overrides the labor rate options.
- Claim submission (5.2.03.07, p.146) and appeals (5.2.02.02, p.144): deadlines apply "unless local laws mandate a longer time-period." FSA claims (6.4.05): state-law period if longer.
- Audits (7.3.02, p.166): no chargeback beyond one year from payment notification unless fraud/false findings "and state law allows going beyond one year."
- Sublet labor cap "unless authorized by law" (5.1.01.13); repair-direction rules don't limit "compensation otherwise required by applicable law" (3.5.02.68, p.83); NPF diagnostic time paid per state statute where one governs diagnostic time (5.1.01.02, p.135).

## Deadlines, audits, chargebacks
- Claims: 30 days from Repair Line Completion; 31–180 days with self-approval code DDET (still subject to review); beyond 180 days only via the Regional Field rep (5.2.03.07–.08; OWS p.64).
- Appeals: 45 days from payment or prior chargeback (5.2.02.02). Ford Lincoln Protect sales-tax claims: 6-month limit (5.2.03.10).
- Records: keep RO and claim support at least one year after Company notification of payment (1.3.02, p.12).
- Chargebacks: any payment not supported, duplicated or not meeting policy can be charged back (5.2.02.01). Audit look-back one year from payment notification, longer only for fraud where state law allows (7.3.02). Audits finding false practices trigger a mandatory follow-up audit within 24 months (7.3.03).
- Unlike Honda/Toyota, the manual has no specific chargeback rule for a labor rate later found above the retail rate.
- WMS Repair Cost Variance (50% of the Dealer Assessment Score) compares parts cost excluding markup and labor hours excluding labor rate (7.2.03, p.163) – a higher rate or markup should not by itself hurt the warranty scorecard.

## OWS User Guide V10.18 (Aug 2026) – relevant items only
- Labor rate and parts markup are system-applied from Ford's dealer records at Pre-Validation; an approved rate change takes effect in OWS, not on the claim. Multiple P&A codes (e.g. secondary rate codes) are switched in a drop-down (p.14).
- DDET extends submission to 180 days from Repair Line Completion; DDET doesn't guarantee full payment (p.64).
- Appeals: only the last submission can be appealed; corrected fields must show the full correct amount, not the difference; follow the manual's time limit (p.155).
- Dealer Action Required / Saved Incomplete claims are cancelled after 30 days of inactivity.
- Rest of the guide is claim-entry mechanics (claim types, codes, parts return) with no rate impact.
